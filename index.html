Research Methodology

From FDA drugs to malaria targets — how it works

We systematically tested whether drugs approved for other diseases could be repurposed against malaria. Here's the full computational pipeline we used to screen and rank candidates.

🖼️ Pipeline diagram — add pipeline.png to the repo root
Step 01
PubChem Search

We queried PubChem for FDA-approved and investigational drugs with known biological targets. This gave us a starting pool of 419 candidate compounds to work with.

Step 02
Target Filtering

From those 419 compounds, we kept only the ones with well-documented protein targets, narrowing the set to 143 drugs ready for the next stage.

Step 03
BLAST Homology

Using BLASTp, we searched for P. falciparum proteins with similar sequences to each drug's original human target. High sequence similarity suggests the drug's binding site may be conserved in the malaria parasite.

Step 04
Structure Alignment

Sequence similarity alone is not enough to confirm a drug will bind. We used UCSF ChimeraX to overlay the 3D structures of each protein pair and verify that the binding pocket geometry is truly compatible.

Step 05
Manual Review

Each candidate pair was reviewed by hand to remove duplicates, low-confidence alignments, and false positives from BLAST. Only reliable drug-target pairs moved forward to docking.

Step 06
Docking Simulation

AutoDock Vina docked each drug into its matched malaria protein binding site. The resulting binding energy score (kcal/mol) measures how tightly the drug fits. Lower scores indicate stronger, more favorable interactions.

Lower score = stronger binding
